Two ways to repay a home loan

Equal payments (annuity): the same amount every month. Early payments are mostly interest; later ones mostly principal. Formula: payment = P × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)−n), where i is the monthly rate and n the number of months.

Equal principal: you repay the same slice of principal each month, plus interest on what is left. The first payment is the highest and each one is a little smaller. Total interest is lower because the balance falls faster.

Example

300,000 at 6% for 25 years: equal payments of about 1,933 a month and about 279,900 total interest. Equal principal starts at 2,500 and ends at 1,005, with about 225,750 total interest, about 54,000 less.

Which to choose

  • Choose equal payments if you want a steady, lower starting payment.
  • Choose equal principal if you can afford higher payments early and want to pay less interest.
  • Either way, extra prepayments early in the loan cut interest the most.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, equal payments or equal principal?

Equal principal, because you repay the loan faster and pay interest on a smaller balance. Equal payments have a lower starting payment.

What is an annuity payment?

A fixed monthly payment that covers interest and principal so the loan is repaid exactly at the end of the term.
