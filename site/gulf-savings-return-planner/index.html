    How the plan is worked out

    Each month, your AED savings are converted to rupees at the rate you enter and added to an investment growing at your expected return. Your monthly savings rise once a year by the percentage you set, reflecting pay rises. If you tick the gratuity box, your UAE end-of-service gratuity at the time you'd leave is added too, using the standard 21/30-day rule.

    Choosing realistic numbers

    • Return: bank fixed deposits in India have recently paid roughly 6–7%; equity funds have historically returned more over long periods but with big swings. Using a lower figure keeps the plan safe.
    • Exchange rate: the rupee has tended to weaken against the dirham over time, which helps Gulf savers, but don't count on it.
    • Inflation: a target of ₹1 crore will buy less in ten years. Consider raising your target for inflation.

    Ways to reach the goal sooner

    Raising monthly savings has the biggest effect, followed by starting early and keeping costs low. Moving money through an NRE account keeps interest tax-free in India while you're an NRI.

    Frequently asked questions

    How long does it take to save ₹1 crore working in the UAE?

    With ₹10 lakh already saved, AED 4,000 saved a month at ₹24 per AED, 7% growth, a 3% yearly increase in savings and gratuity on an AED 8,000 basic salary, it takes about 5 years. Use the planner with your own numbers.

    Should I include gratuity in my savings plan?

    Yes, if you expect to receive it. It is often a large lump sum after several years of service.
