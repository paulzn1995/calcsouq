    How a SIP grows

    A systematic investment plan (SIP) puts the same amount into a fund every month. Each instalment then grows for the months that remain. With a step-up, the monthly amount rises by a fixed percentage once a year, usually in line with salary increases.

    Example

    10,000 a month for 15 years at an assumed 12% a year: flat, about 18 lakh invested grows to roughly 50 lakh. With a 10% yearly step-up, about 38 lakh invested grows to roughly 87 lakh. Use the calculator for exact figures.

    Points to keep in mind

    • Equity fund returns vary year to year and can be negative; the rate you enter is an assumption, not a promise.
    • Fund expenses and taxes on gains reduce what you keep.
    • Starting earlier usually matters more than the amount.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is SIP return calculated?

    Each monthly instalment is compounded monthly at the assumed annual rate ÷ 12 until the end of the period; the values are added together.

    What is a step-up SIP?

    A SIP whose monthly amount increases by a set percentage each year, for example 10%.

    Is the result guaranteed?

    No. It is an estimate based on the return you enter. Actual fund returns vary.
