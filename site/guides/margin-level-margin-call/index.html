Margin level, margin call and stop out explained

Four numbers in your MT5 terminal decide whether your positions stay open.

Try the Forex Margin Calculator →Required margin for any lot size and leverage
  • Balance: account cash, excluding open trades.
  • Equity: balance plus or minus open profit and loss.
  • Margin (used): the deposit locked by open positions.
  • Free margin: equity − used margin, the amount available for new trades.
  • Margin level: equity ÷ used margin × 100%.

Worked example

Balance $2,000. You open 1 lot of EUR/USD at 1.0850 with 1:100 leverage, so used margin is $1,085. If the trade is down $600, equity is $1,400 and margin level = 1,400 ÷ 1,085 = 129%.

If the loss grows to $1,500, equity is $500 and margin level is 46%. With a 50% stop-out level, the broker starts closing positions.

Margin call vs stop out

A margin call is a warning level (often 100%). The stop out is where positions are closed automatically, largest loser first. Both levels are set by your broker.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good margin level?

Many traders try to stay well above 300–500% so normal price swings never approach the stop-out level.

What happens at stop out?

The broker automatically closes positions, usually the biggest loser first, until the margin level is back above the limit.
