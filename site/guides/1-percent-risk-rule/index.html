The 1% risk rule

The 1% rule says a single losing trade should cost no more than 1% of your account.

Try the Position Size Calculator →Lot size from account risk and stop loss

Why it works

Every strategy has losing streaks. Small risk per trade means a streak hurts but doesn't end your trading.

Losses in a row1% risk2% risk5% risk10% risk
5−5%−10%−23%−41%
10−10%−18%−40%−65%
15−14%−26%−54%−79%
20−18%−33%−64%−88%

After 10 straight losses, a 1% trader is down about 10%; a 10% trader has lost about two thirds of the account and needs nearly a 200% gain just to get back to the start.

Applying it

  1. Risk amount = account × 1%.
  2. Place your stop where the trade idea is proven wrong.
  3. Lot size = risk amount ÷ (stop distance × value per pip or point).

Spreads, commission and slippage can make actual losses slightly larger, so some traders use 0.5–1%.

Frequently asked questions

Is 2% risk per trade too much?

Many professionals use 0.5–2%. At 2%, ten straight losses cost about 18% of the account.

Does the 1% rule mean a 1% stop loss?

No. It limits the money lost, not the price distance. The stop distance decides the position size.
