Do you get full gratuity if you resign in the UAE?

Under the old law, resigning early could cut your gratuity by a third or two thirds. That rule is gone.

Updated 25 Nov 2026

Use the UAE Gratuity Calculator →End-of-service benefit under the new labour law

The rule today

Under Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, private-sector employees receive the same gratuity whether they resign or are terminated: 21 days' basic pay per year for the first five years and 30 days per year after that, provided they completed at least one year of service.

What the old law did

On the old unlimited contracts, resigning after 1–3 years earned only one third of the gratuity, and 3–5 years earned two thirds. Those reductions do not appear in the new law.

Dismissal without notice

The new law also changed summary dismissal. Under Article 44 an employer can dismiss without notice in ten specific cases, but the end-of-service gratuity is still payable. Under the old Article 120 it could be forfeited. Your employer may deduct amounts you genuinely owe.

What you still need to do

  • Serve the notice period in your contract, between 30 and 90 days.
  • Your employer must pay all dues within 14 days of the end of the contract.
  • Keep your offer letter and payslips showing your basic salary.

If dues are not paid, you can file a complaint with MOHRE through its app or website.

Frequently asked questions

Is gratuity reduced if I resign in UAE?

No. Under the new labour law there is no reduction for resigning.

Do I get gratuity if I am fired without notice?

Yes. Under the new law, gratuity is payable even after a dismissal under Article 44, though proven amounts owed to the employer can be deducted.

How long does the employer have to pay gratuity?

All end-of-service dues must be paid within 14 days of the end of the employment contract.
