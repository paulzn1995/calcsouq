How much does a 0.25% interest rate rise cost on a home loan?

A quarter-point rise sounds tiny. On a long loan it adds a small amount each month and a surprisingly large amount in total.

Updated 25 Nov 2026

Use the Loan EMI Calculator (AED & ₹) →Monthly instalment and total interest

The short answer

On a 20-year loan, moving from 8.50% to 8.75% raises the EMI by about ₹159 per ₹10 lakh borrowed. For a ₹5,000,000 loan that is ₹794 a month, or ₹190,650 extra over the full 20 years, if you keep the tenure unchanged.

Worked example

A quick shortcut is 0.25% of the loan divided by 12: ₹5,000,000 × 0.25% ÷ 12 = ₹1,042. The true increase is lower, ₹794, because the extra interest is spread over 240 payments while the balance falls. The shortcut is a fair upper bound for long loans.

The real figure for you depends on three things: your outstanding balance, the months left, and whether the lender raises the EMI or extends the tenure. Extending the tenure costs more in total; see our guide on higher EMI or longer tenure.

Why rates are in the news

In India, the Reserve Bank raised the repo rate by 0.25 points to 5.50% in October 2026, and October retail inflation came in at 6.09%, above the 6% upper tolerance band. The next policy decision is due on 4 December 2026. Loans linked to an external benchmark such as the repo rate usually reset at the next reset date set in your loan agreement, not on the day of the announcement.

Elsewhere the same arithmetic applies to any floating-rate mortgage: put your balance, new rate and remaining term into the calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How much does EMI increase for a 0.25% rate rise on ₹50 lakh?

About ₹794 a month on a 20-year loan moving from 8.50% to 8.75%, if the tenure stays the same.

When will my EMI change after a repo rate rise?

Repo-linked loans change on the reset date in your loan agreement, often the start of the next month or quarter. Your lender will confirm the new rate.

Does a 0.25% rise cost more on a longer loan?

Yes. The longer the remaining term, the more months the higher rate applies to, so the total extra interest is larger.
