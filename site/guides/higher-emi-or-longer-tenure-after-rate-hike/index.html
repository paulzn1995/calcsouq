After a rate hike, should you pay a higher EMI or extend the tenure?

Keeping the same EMI feels painless, but every extra month of tenure is a month of interest. Paying the higher EMI almost always costs less in total.

Updated 24 Nov 2026

Use the Loan EMI Calculator (AED & ₹) →Monthly instalment and total interest

The short answer

If your budget can take it, choose the higher EMI. The loan ends on the original date and you pay less interest. Extending the tenure keeps the instalment the same, but the extra months are paid at the new, higher rate, so the total cost rises by much more than the EMI increase suggests.

Many banks extend the tenure by default on floating-rate loans, because it needs no change to your standing instruction. Check your latest loan statement for the remaining tenure, not only the EMI.

Worked example: ₹50 lakh over 20 years

Loan of ₹5,000,000 at 8.5% for 20 years. EMI ₹43,391, total interest ₹5,413,879. Now the rate rises at the very start. You can either pay a higher EMI for the same 20 years (option A) or keep paying ₹43,391 for longer (option B).

Calculated with the standard reducing-balance EMI formula; the rate change is applied to the full balance from month one. Real loans part-way through will see smaller numbers, but the same pattern.

What the table shows

A 0.25 percentage point rise adds only ₹794 a month to the EMI. Keeping the old EMI instead adds about 13 months to the loan and ₹348,886 more interest than option A. After three such rises the tenure grows by more than three years.

The reason is simple: with a fixed EMI, a larger share of each payment goes to interest, so the balance falls more slowly. Close to the point where the EMI only covers the interest, small rate rises push the tenure out sharply.

When extending can make sense

  • Your monthly budget truly cannot absorb the higher EMI.
  • You expect a bonus or lump sum and plan to prepay soon, which shortens the tenure again.
  • You are young enough that a longer loan still ends well before retirement. Banks usually cap the loan at a maximum age.

A middle path

Keep the old EMI but make a small part-prepayment once a year, or ask the bank to raise the EMI by a part of the increase. Use the calculator with your outstanding balance, new rate and remaining months to see both options before you call your lender.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my home loan tenure increase instead of my EMI?

On floating-rate loans many lenders keep the EMI unchanged and extend the tenure when rates rise. You can usually ask them to raise the EMI instead; check your loan agreement and the lender's process.

Is a higher EMI or a longer tenure cheaper after a rate hike?

A higher EMI. In our ₹50 lakh, 20-year example, a 0.25 point rise costs ₹348,886 more in interest if you extend the tenure instead.

Can I switch back to a higher EMI later?

Usually yes, by request to the lender or by making part-prepayments. In India, RBI rules bar prepayment charges on floating-rate loans taken by individuals for non-business purposes; elsewhere, check your loan terms.

Does this apply to loans outside India?

Yes. The maths is the same for any reducing-balance loan in any currency; only the rates and lender rules differ.
