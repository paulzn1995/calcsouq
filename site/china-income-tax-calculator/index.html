    How salary tax is worked out in China

    Employers withhold tax each month using the cumulative method: from January, add up salary, minus 5,000 yuan a month, minus your social insurance and housing fund, minus special additional deductions. Tax on that running total uses the annual brackets, and each month you pay the difference from last month. That is why tax often rises later in the year as you move into a higher bracket.

    Special additional deductions (per month)

    • Child education and children under 3: 2,000 yuan per child.
    • Supporting parents aged 60 or over: 3,000 yuan for an only child; siblings share 3,000, at most 1,500 each.
    • First-home loan interest 1,000 yuan, or rent 1,500, 1,100 or 800 yuan depending on city size.
    • Degree-level continuing education 400 yuan.

    Example

    Salary 20,000 yuan, 22.5% social insurance and housing fund (4,500), no special deductions: taxable income is 10,500 a month, 126,000 a year. Tax for the year is 126,000 × 10% − 2,520 = 10,080 yuan, about 840 a month on average.

    Contribution bases, caps and rates differ by city. Year-end bonuses can be taxed separately; this calculator covers monthly salary.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the tax-free threshold in China?

    5,000 yuan a month (60,000 a year), before social insurance, housing fund and special additional deductions.

    Why is my tax higher in later months?

    Withholding is cumulative. As your year-to-date taxable income crosses a bracket, the rate on new income goes up.

    How much can I deduct for children?

    2,000 yuan a month per child in education, and 2,000 per child under 3. Parents can split it 50/50 or one parent takes 100%.
