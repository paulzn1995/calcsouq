    How margin works

    Margin is the deposit your broker holds while a leveraged position is open. With 1:100 leverage you put up 1% of the position's value. One standard lot of EUR/USD at 1.0850 is worth $108,500, so the margin at 1:100 is $1,085.

    Leverage limits

    Regulators cap retail leverage. In the UAE, the SCA sets limits by asset class for retail clients, and EU and UK rules cap major FX pairs at 1:30 for retail traders. Your broker's own margin requirements may be higher than this calculator shows, especially over weekends and news events.

    Free margin and margin level

    Margin level = equity ÷ used margin × 100%. Most brokers send a margin call around 100% and start closing positions at a stop-out level of 50% or lower.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much margin do I need for 1 lot of EUR/USD?

    At 1:100 leverage and a price of 1.0850, you need about $1,085. At 1:30 you need about $3,617.

    How is margin calculated for gold?

    One lot of XAU/USD is 100 oz. At $2,650 the position is worth $265,000, so at 1:100 the margin is $2,650.

    What happens if my margin level falls too low?

    You may get a margin call, and below the stop-out level the broker will automatically close positions.
