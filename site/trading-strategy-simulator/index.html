    Why simulate instead of using averages

    Expectancy, meaning win rate × reward minus loss rate × risk, tells you whether a strategy makes money on average. It doesn't tell you how rough the ride will be. Two traders with the same edge can have very different months purely through the order of wins and losses. This simulator plays out your next trades 1,000 times with random order, so you can see the spread of outcomes.

    How to read the results

    • Typical worst drawdown: the peak-to-trough fall in half of the runs. Expect at least this.
    • Bad-luck drawdown (1 in 20): plan your risk so you could survive this.
    • Losing streak: even a 50% win rate regularly produces runs of 6–8 losses in 200 trades.
    • Drawdown limit: the share of runs that hit your limit, for example a prop-firm's maximum loss from the starting balance.

    Worked example

    Win rate 45%, reward 2R, costs 0.05R: expectancy = 0.45 × 2 − 0.55 − 0.05 = +0.30R per trade. At 1% risk that edge is solid, but the simulator typically still shows double-digit drawdowns along the way.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a Monte Carlo simulation in trading?

    It replays your strategy many times in random order to show the range of possible results, drawdowns and losing streaks, not just the average.

    How do I lower my drawdown?

    Lower the risk per trade. Halving risk roughly halves drawdowns, and expectancy per trade stays the same.
