    Compound interest formula

    A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)n × t, where P is the deposit, r the annual rate, n the number of times interest is added each year and t the number of years. The more often interest is compounded, the higher the effective annual rate.

    Example: a 5-year fixed deposit

    1,00,000 at 7% a year, compounded quarterly: 1,00,000 × (1 + 0.07 ÷ 4)20 = 1,41,478. Interest earned is 41,478 and the effective annual rate is 7.19%.

    Things that change the real return

    • Tax on interest (in India, FD interest is added to your income; TDS may be deducted).
    • Some banks pay interest out quarterly instead of reinvesting it; then use simple interest.
    • Breaking a deposit early usually reduces the rate.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is FD maturity calculated?

    With compound interest: principal × (1 + rate ÷ compounding periods) raised to the number of periods. Most Indian bank FDs compound quarterly.

    What is the effective annual rate?

    The yearly return after compounding. 7% compounded quarterly equals 7.19% a year.

    Does this include tax?

    No. Results are before tax.
